Project Case Study · 02
CabbageTrack

A vision-only UAV pipeline that recognises individual plants again on later drone passes, from the video alone: no RTK-GPS, no ground-control points, no landmarks.

RoleIndependent Research Project
StackPython · YOLOv9c-seg · OpenCV
Result98.9% of blind plants re-identified (held-out flight)

Overview

Tracking the same individual plant across repeated drone surveys is the basis for per-plant growth curves, early stress detection, and yield forecasting. The standard solution, RTK-GPS on the drone, is accurate but expensive and often out of reach for smallholder or commercial vegetable operations on tighter budgets. CabbageTrack asks whether that same re-identification can be done from imagery alone.

The pipeline segments plants with YOLOv9c-seg, tracks them within each pass with BoT-SORT, and follows the drone's movement by phase correlation of consecutive video frames, a keypoint-free technique that suits repetitive crop texture. Because that path drifts, it is used only to find earlier views of the same ground. For each such view, the shift between the two images is measured directly and the remembered plants are moved by it, then matched to the new plant tracks with Hungarian assignment.

It was developed on three flights over cabbage and cauliflower, frozen, and run once on a held-out flight over a new cabbage field, where it re-identified 90 of 91 plants sampled blindly by human annotators. The full methodology is described in a manuscript currently in revision at Computers and Electronics in Agriculture.

Major Findings

  1. On the held-out flight, 90 of 91 blind plants were re-identified (98.9%) and 601 of the method's 611 links between passes were judged correct (98.4%), with no GPS or ground-control points involved.
  2. Measuring the shift between the two views at each revisit, instead of trusting the drifting camera map, lifted re-identification from 74.5% to 93.3% over 549 blind plants on four flights. It is the decisive component, not a stronger plant descriptor.
  3. Using only the drone's own GNSS log, 61.7% of blind plants were re-identified, against 93.3% for the video-only method: the logged height refers to the take-off point, not the crop, so the image scale comes out wrong. GNSS and vision are complementary rather than rivals.
  4. The camera path built from video alone stayed within 0.11–0.71 m (RMSE) of the GNSS track, and the clear limit is a flight where two passes share only about a quarter of the image (43 of 74 plants re-identified).
